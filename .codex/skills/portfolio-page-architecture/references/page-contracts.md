# Landing-page contracts

Use the smallest structure that tells the truth about the work. A section is optional only when its question has no evidence or would repeat another section. Never fill a missing chapter with generic marketing copy.

## Home

1. **Page statement:** Who Raúl is and the kind of work he builds.
2. **Selected work:** A small, legible route into case studies, apps, visuals, or photography.
3. **Operating perspective:** How strategy, systems, visual judgement, and technology relate.
4. **Services or collaborations:** What a visitor can engage Raúl for, expressed as concrete work rather than capability clouds.
5. **Contact:** A direct invitation with a realistic next action.

The home page is the visual reference, not a layout every other page must clone.

## Case-study index

1. **Intro:** Visible H1 and a one-sentence explanation of the work represented.
2. **Collection:** A thumbnail-led, accessible grid. Every item names itself without relying on image recognition.
3. **Navigation:** Optional filter or category mechanism only when it shortens a real search task.
4. **Continuation:** Related practice or contact after the collection when it helps the visitor choose a path.

Vary image aspect ratios only when the variation comes from the work. Keep captions and hover/focus behaviour consistent across the grid.

## Service landing

1. **Direct answer:** What the service is, who it helps, and its practical boundary.
2. **Context:** The recurring problem, constraint, or decision that makes the service useful.
3. **Deliverables:** Concrete artifacts, working sessions, systems, or outputs.
4. **Application:** Situations where this work is appropriate, with proof where available.
5. **Process:** How the engagement moves from input to reviewable output.
6. **Related proof:** Relevant case studies, never an unrelated portfolio dump.
7. **FAQ and contact:** Only questions that resolve a genuine decision; finish with a clear inquiry action.

## About

1. **Identity:** Name, current practice, and a concise position.
2. **Evidence of practice:** A few concrete signals, not a wall of credentials.
3. **Context:** Geography, timeline, or working history only where it explains the current work.
4. **Operating model:** How Raúl approaches a decision, workflow, interface, and review.
5. **Routes into the work:** Selected case studies, apps, visuals, or photography.
6. **Contact:** A human, low-friction next step.

The page may be personal, but it should still give a visitor an editorial reading path. Avoid turning it into a dashboard of tags, timelines, stats, and cards at once.

## App or product landing

1. **Job and user:** What narrow job the product helps with.
2. **Product proof:** One or more real states, annotated by the decision each state supports.
3. **Workflow:** Inputs, key steps, human judgment, and outputs.
4. **Boundary:** What the product does not do, risks, or constraints when relevant.
5. **Action:** Live product, demo, repository, related case study, or contact.

Screens are evidence. Do not make a generic product-marketing hero with a floating dashboard, fabricated metrics, and decorative feature cards.

## Photography and visuals

1. **Practice statement:** What is being shown and the editorial or visual thread that connects it.
2. **Work selection:** The image sequence or collection itself, with a readable way to enter and leave it.
3. **Context when useful:** Series, place, year, material, process, or commission notes.
4. **Continuation:** Another body of work or contact.

The visual work can be more immersive, but headers, labels, reading copy, media captions, controls, and the footer remain part of the shared portfolio system.

## Quiet utility pages

Legal, support, loading, empty, error, and not-found routes do not need editorial spectacle. They still use readable measure, visible headings, shared colour, focus treatment, and a clear way back into the portfolio.
